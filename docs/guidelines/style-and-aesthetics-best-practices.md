# Style and Aesthetics Best Practices for Media Studio

## Overview

This document provides comprehensive best practices for agents working with style and aesthetics in Media Studio. It covers visual style development, color theory, lighting techniques, cinematic composition, and maintaining aesthetic consistency across video productions.

## Core Principles

### 1. **Consistency Over Variety**
- **Visual Unity**: Maintain consistent visual language across all shots
- **Style Discipline**: Resist the urge to constantly change visual style
- **Reference Adherence**: Stick to approved visual references
- **Genre Alignment**: Ensure style matches the production's genre

### 2. **Purpose-Driven Aesthetics**
- **Story-First**: Every aesthetic choice should serve the narrative
- **Emotional Resonance**: Style should enhance emotional tone
- **Character Expression**: Visual style should support character development
- **Temporal Consistency**: Style should be appropriate for the time period

### 3. **Quality Over Novelty**
- **Technical Excellence**: Prioritize technical quality over novelty
- **Timeless Aesthetics**: Choose enduring styles over trendy ones
- **Professional Standards**: Maintain broadcast-quality standards
- **Subtle Refinement**: Prefer subtle refinement over radical changes

## Visual Style Development

### 1. **Style Definition Process**

#### **Initial Style Definition**
```markdown
Style Development Workflow:
1. Analyze script requirements and genre
2. Research reference films and visual inspirations
3. Define core color palette and mood
4. Establish lighting approach
5. Set technical parameters (resolution, format)
6. Create style guide documentation
7. Generate reference images
8. Iterate based on feedback
```

#### **Style Guide Components**
- **Color Palette**: Primary, secondary, and accent colors
- **Lighting Approach**: Natural vs. artificial, key vs. fill ratios
- **Camera Style**: Preferred framings, movements, lenses
- **Texture and Material**: Surface qualities and material properties
- **Atmosphere**: Overall mood and feeling
- **Technical Specs**: Resolution, format, codec requirements

### 2. **Color Theory for Video**

#### **Color Palette Best Practices**
- **Primary Colors**: 2-3 dominant colors that define the visual identity
- **Secondary Colors**: 3-4 supporting colors that complement primary colors
- **Accent Colors**: 1-2 colors for emphasis and highlights
- **Neutral Colors**: Black, white, gray tones for balance
- **Skin Tone Accuracy**: Ensure realistic and diverse skin tones

#### **Color Harmony Rules**
```markdown
Effective Color Harmonies:
- Monochromatic: Single hue with varying saturation/brightness
- Analogous: Adjacent colors on color wheel (e.g., blue, blue-green, green)
- Complementary: Opposite colors for contrast (e.g., blue-orange)
- Split-Complementary: Base color with two adjacent to its complement
- Triadic: Three evenly spaced colors on color wheel
- Tetradic: Four colors in two complementary pairs
```

#### **Color Temperature Guidelines**
- **Warm Tones**: Orange, red, yellow → evoke energy, passion, intimacy
- **Cool Tones**: Blue, green, purple → evoke calm, melancholy, professionalism
- **Neutral Tones**: Grays, browns → ground the scene, provide balance
- **Mixed Temperature**: Warm/cool contrast creates visual interest
- **Scene-Appropriate Choice**: Match color temperature to scene mood

### 3. **Lighting Techniques**

#### **Natural Lighting Simulation**
```markdown
Natural Lighting Scenarios:
Golden Hour: Warm, soft, long shadows (drama, romance)
Midday: Bright, harsh, minimal shadows (comedy, documentary)
Blue Hour: Cool, soft, blue-tinted (melancholy, mystery)
Overcast: Diffuse, flat, low contrast (neutral, somber)
Night: Low key, high contrast, colored shadows (thriller, noir)
```

#### **Studio Lighting Approaches**
- **Three-Point Lighting**: Key light, fill light, back light for depth
- **Rembrandt Lighting**: Strong key light, limited fill, dramatic shadows
- **High Key Lighting**: Bright, even illumination, minimal shadows
- **Low Key Lighting**: Dominant shadows, selective illumination
- **Rim Lighting**: Back lighting for character outline and separation

#### **Lighting Quality Guidelines**
- **Hard Light**: Sharp shadows, dramatic contrast (thriller, drama)
- **Soft Light**: Diffuse shadows, gentle contrast (romance, comedy)
- **Mixed Lighting**: Combine hard and soft for visual interest
- **Directional Light**: Emphasize texture and form
- **Ambient Light**: Provide base illumination without harsh shadows

### 4. **Cinematic Composition**

#### **Shot Framing Best Practices**
```markdown
Standard Framing Guidelines:
Extreme Close-Up: Face detail, emotional intensity (0.5-2 sec)
Close-Up: Head and shoulders, dialogue emphasis (2-5 sec)
Medium Close-Up: Chest up, character presence (3-7 sec)
Medium Shot: Waist up, character action (5-10 sec)
Medium Long Shot: Full body, context and action (5-15 sec)
Long Shot: Full scene, establishing (5-15 sec)
Extreme Long Shot: Wide establishing (5-15 sec)
```

#### **Camera Movement Guidelines**
- **Static**: No movement, focus on performance
- **Pan**: Horizontal movement, reveals or follows action
- **Tilt**: Vertical movement, reveal or emphasize
- **Dolly**: Camera moves toward/away from subject
- **Truck**: Camera moves sideways, follows action
- **Pedestal**: Camera moves up/down, reveals scale
- **Handheld**: Slight shake for documentary feel
- **Steadic**: Smooth movement for cinematic feel

#### **Composition Rules**
- **Rule of Thirds**: Place key elements on grid intersections
- **Leading Lines**: Use lines to guide viewer's eye
- **Framing**: Leave appropriate headroom and noseroom
- **Depth**: Use foreground/background layers for depth
- **Balance**: Distribute visual weight appropriately
- **Focus**: Use shallow depth of field for emphasis

## Genre-Specific Aesthetics

### 1. **Narrative Film**
- **Naturalistic Lighting**: Mimic natural light sources
- **Realistic Color Palette**: Earth tones, natural colors
- **Conventional Framing**: Standard cinematic framings
- **Smooth Camera Movement**: Gentle, motivated camera motion
- **Character-Focused**: Prioritize character over environment

### 2. **Documentary**
- **Natural Lighting**: Embrace available light conditions
- **Handheld Camera**: Slight movement for authenticity
- **Spontaneous Framing**: Adaptable to real situations
- **Realistic Color**: Natural colors, minimal grading
- **Interview Framing**: Rule of thirds, headroom, eye-level

### 3. **Thriller/Horror**
- **High Contrast Lighting**: Dramatic shadows, chiaroscuro
- **Cool Color Palette**: Blues, greens, dark tones
- **Low Key Lighting**: Selective illumination, darkness
- **Unstable Framing**: Slight tilt, unconventional angles
- **Suspenseful Pacing**: Slow build, quick reveals

### 4. **Comedy**
- **Bright Lighting**: High key, even illumination
- **Warm Color Palette**: Cheerful, inviting colors
- **Dynamic Framing**: Active camera movement
- **Expressive Acting**: Over-the-top performances
- **Fast Pacing**: Quick cuts, energetic timing

### 5. **Period Piece**
- **Historical Accuracy**: Research period-appropriate aesthetics
- **Period Lighting**: Match technology era (candlelight, gaslight, electric)
- **Authentic Color Palette**: Period-appropriate color grading
- **Traditional Framing**: Classical composition rules
- **Period Props**: Authentic props and settings

### 6. **Science Fiction**
- **Futuristic Lighting**: Colored lights, neon effects
- **Unusual Color Palette**: Unnatural colors, high saturation
- **High-Tech Elements**: Clean lines, advanced technology
- **Innovative Framing**: Creative camera angles
- **Speculative Design**: Original visual concepts

## Style Guide Development

### 1. **Style Bible Structure**

#### **Comprehensive Style Guide**
```markdown
Style Bible Components:
1. Executive Summary: Production overview and visual direction

3. Lighting Approach: Lighting plan, fixture types, setups
```

### 2. **Visual Reference Management**

#### **Reference Image Curation**
- **Character References**: Full-body and close-up character shots
- **Location References**: Establishing shots and detail shots
- **Mood Boards**: Color palettes, lighting examples, atmosphere
- **Technical References**: Camera setups, lighting diagrams

#### **Reference Approval Process**
```markdown
Reference Approval Workflow:
1. Generate reference based on style guide
2. Review against aesthetic requirements

4. Verify character/location accuracy
5. Get user approval
6. Mark as canonical reference
7. Use for all subsequent renders
```

## Aesthetic Consistency

### 1. **Temporal Consistency**

#### **Time-of-Day Handling**
```markdown
Time-of-Day Aesthetic Guidelines:

Morning: Bright, crisp, moderate shadows
Midday: Bright, harsh, minimal shadows
Afternoon: Warm, directional, lengthening shadows
Golden Hour: Warm, soft, golden light, dramatic shadows
```

#### **Weather Conditions**
- **Sunny**: Bright, high contrast, defined shadows
- **Overcast**: Diffuse, flat, low contrast
- **Rainy**: Wet surfaces, reflections, desaturated colors
- **Foggy**: Hazy, low contrast, muted colors
- **Snowy**: Bright, high contrast, blue tint

#### **Location Consistency**
- **Location Matching**: Ensure locations look consistent across scenes
- **Weather Continuity**: Maintain weather conditions within scenes
- **Lighting Consistency**: Keep lighting consistent within scene
- **Props Continuity**: Ensure props appear/disappear appropriately
- **Costume Continuity**: Maintain costume continuity across scenes

#### **Character Consistency**
- **Appearance Consistency**: Maintain character appearance across shots
- **Costume Continuity**: Ensure costume changes are logical
- **Makeup Continuity**: Maintain makeup consistency within scenes
- **Behavior Consistency**: Maintain character behavior patterns
- **Expression Consistency**: Keep emotional expressions appropriate

### 3. **Technical Consistency**

#### **Resolution and Format**
- **Resolution Consistency**: Maintain consistent resolution throughout
- **Aspect Ratio**: Keep aspect ratio consistent (16:9, 2.39:1, etc.)
- **Frame Rate**: Maintain consistent frame rate (24fps, 30fps, 60fps)
- **Codec Quality**: Maintain consistent encoding quality
- **Bitrate**: Maintain consistent bitrate within tolerances

#### **Quality Standards**
- **Sharpness**: Consistent focus and sharpness standards
- **Noise Levels**: Maintain consistent noise/grain levels
- **Color Accuracy**: Maintain consistent color grading
- **Exposure**: Maintain consistent exposure levels
- **White Balance**: Maintain consistent white balance

## Agent Decision-Making for Aesthetics

### 1. **Style Adjustment Guidelines**

#### **When to Suggest Style Changes**
```markdown
Style Change Indicators:
- Current style doesn't match genre expectations
- User feedback indicates style doesn't work
- Technical limitations prevent current style execution
- Story evolution requires style shift
- Market or audience feedback suggests change
```

#### **Style Change Process**
1. **Analyze Current Style**: Document what's working and what isn't
2. **Propose Alternatives**: Suggest specific style adjustments
3. **Show Examples**: Provide visual examples of proposed changes
4. **User Approval**: Get user approval before implementing
5. **Gradual Transition**: Implement changes gradually
6. **Validation**: Verify changes improve results

### 2. **Aesthetic Problem Solving**

#### **Common Aesthetic Issues**
```markdown
Issue Diagnosis and Solutions:
Color Inconsistency: Re-establish color palette, adjust lighting
Lighting Mismatch: Verify time-of-day, adjust light sources
Composition Problems: Revisit framing rules, camera placement
Character Inconsistency: Check references, adjust character prompts
Quality Variations: Adjust technical parameters, re-render
```

#### **Quality Assessment Framework**
- **Color Accuracy**: Check color grading against style guide
- **Lighting Quality**: Evaluate lighting technique and effectiveness
- **Framing**: Check composition against established rules
- **Focus**: Verify sharpness and depth of field
- **Consistency**: Check against adjacent shots for continuity

### 3. **User Feedback Integration**

#### **Aesthetic Feedback Processing**
```markdown
Feedback Integration Workflow:
1. Collect User Feedback: Gather specific aesthetic comments
2. Categorize Feedback: Color, lighting, framing, etc.
3. Validate Feedback: Check against style guide and best practices
4. Implement Changes: Make specific adjustments based on feedback
5. Validate Results: Verify changes address feedback
6. Document Decisions: Record aesthetic decisions for future reference
```

#### **Feedback Categories**
- **Color Grading**: "Too blue", "too saturated", "not enough contrast"
- **Lighting**: "Too dark", "too bright", "wrong mood"
- **Framing**: "Too tight", "too wide", "wrong angle"
- **Movement**: "Too shaky", "too fast", "unmotivated"
- **Style**: "Doesn't match genre", "inconsistent with previous shots"

## Common Aesthetic Mistakes

### 1. **Color Mistakes**

#### **Common Color Issues**
- **Inconsistent White Balance**: Unintended color casts
- **Oversaturation**: Colors look unnatural or garish
- **Color Drift**: Colors change between shots
- **Poor Color Harmony**: Colors clash or don't work together
- **Ignoring Color Temperature**: Unintended warm/cool mismatch

#### **Solutions**
- **Establish White Balance**: Set consistent white balance
- **Use Color Palettes**: Stick to defined color palettes
- **Color Check**: Verify colors match adjacent shots
- **Harmony Rules**: Apply color harmony principles
- **Temperature Awareness**: Consider color temperature in prompts

### 2. **Lighting Mistakes**

#### **Common Lighting Issues**
- **Inconsistent Lighting**: Lighting changes between shots
- **Wrong Mood**: Lighting doesn't match scene mood
- **Over-Lighting**: Scene looks artificially bright
- **Under-Lighting**: Scene is too dark to see clearly
- **Unmotivated Light Sources**: Light sources don't make sense

#### **Solutions**
- **Lighting Consistency**: Maintain lighting within scenes
- **Mood Alignment**: Match lighting to scene mood
- **Light Budgeting**: Use appropriate light intensity
- **Shadow Management**: Ensure shadows are intentional
- **Source Logic**: Ensure light sources make story sense

### 3. **Composition Mistakes**

#### **Common Composition Issues**
- **Poor Framing**: Heads cut off, bad headroom
- **Awkward Angles: Uncomfortable camera angles
- **Unstable Framing**: Unmotivated camera movement
- **Weak Composition**: Lacking visual interest
- **Breaking Rules**: Breaking composition rules without purpose

#### **Solutions**
- **Framing Guidelines**: Follow standard framing rules
- **Angle Justification**: Ensure camera angles are motivated
- **Movement Motivation**: Ensure camera movement has purpose
- **Composition Principles**: Apply composition rules
- **Rule Breaking**: Break rules only for specific effect
